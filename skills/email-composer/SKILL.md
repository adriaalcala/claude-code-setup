# Email Composer Skill

## Overview
Draft professional, clear, and appropriate emails in both English and Spanish. Covers common business scenarios with templates and best practices.

## Phase 1: ASSESS

### Understand Requirements
- **Recipient**: Who are you writing to? (colleague, manager, client, external contact)
- **Purpose**: What's the primary goal? (request, update, apology, introduction, scheduling, deadline)
- **Tone**: Formal, semi-formal, friendly, urgent?
- **Language**: English or Spanish?
- **Context**: Is this first contact or ongoing relationship?
- **Urgency**: Normal, important, or time-sensitive?

### Identify Email Type
- Meeting request
- Project update
- Deadline reminder
- Introduction
- Apology
- Thank you
- Problem escalation
- Decision notification
- Feedback request

## Phase 2: DRAFT

### Structure
All professional emails follow:
1. **Greeting** — appropriate for relationship level
2. **Opening** — context, why you're writing
3. **Body** — main content (2-3 focused paragraphs)
4. **Call-to-Action** — what response/action needed (if applicable)
5. **Closing** — professional sign-off
6. **Signature** — name, title, contact info

### Best Practices
- Keep paragraphs short (3-4 sentences max)
- Use clear subject lines that describe content
- Avoid all-caps (appears like shouting)
- Avoid vague language ("ASAP", "soon", "shortly")
- Use specific deadlines ("by Friday 5pm")
- Be concise but warm
- Passive voice only when necessary (focus on actions, not blame)

### Common Phrases by Situation

**Requests**
- "Could you please..."
- "I would appreciate if you could..."
- "Would it be possible to..."
- "I'm reaching out regarding..."

**Urgency**
- "I wanted to flag this because..."
- "This requires your attention by..."
- "This is time-sensitive..."

**Agreement/Positive**
- "Thank you for..."
- "I appreciate your..."
- "Great idea about..."

**Disagreement (tactful)**
- "I had another thought on..."
- "Have you considered..."
- "I see your point, and I'd also suggest..."

**Next Steps**
- "Let's connect on..."
- "I'll send over... by..."
- "Looking forward to your thoughts"

## Phase 3: REFINE

Check for:

### Clarity
- Is the purpose clear in first paragraph?
- Does each sentence add value?
- Would a non-expert understand this?

### Tone & Professionalism
- Any accidental harshness or passive-aggressive tone?
- Appropriate formality for relationship?
- Typos, grammar, spelling?

### Ambiguity
- Could key points be misinterpreted?
- Are deadlines and expectations clear?
- Is call-to-action explicit?

### Length
- Too long? (more than 1 screen = too long)
- Too short? (missing context?)
- Ideal: 100-300 words

### Passive Voice
- Minimize "is", "was", "be"
- Use active verbs: "I reviewed", "We decided", "Please send"

## Phase 4: PRESENT

Show draft to user with:
- Plain text version ready to copy
- 1-2 alternative variants (if major tone choices)
- Summary of what it accomplishes
- Suggested subject line(s)
- Invite refinement ("Would you like me to adjust...?")

## Template Library

### Meeting Request (English)

**Subject**: Meeting Request - [Topic]

Dear [Name],

I hope this email finds you well. I wanted to connect regarding [specific topic], as I think it would be valuable for us to align on [brief context].

Would you be available for a 30-minute meeting next week? I'm flexible with timing and can work around your schedule. Some times that work for me are:
- Tuesday, 2-4 PM
- Wednesday, 10 AM-12 PM
- Thursday, 1-3 PM

Let me know what works best for you, or feel free to suggest an alternative time.

Thanks,
[Your Name]

## Writing Tips

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Using "hope you're doing well" in every email (varies if ongoing correspondence)
- Ending with a period after sign-off (use comma after greeting)
- Multiple exclamation marks (comes across as unprofessional)
- Using emoticons or slang
- Forwarding without adding context
- "Per my last email" (frustrating tone)

### Tone Checklist
- [ ] Not too formal? (check for unnecessary jargon)
- [ ] Not too casual? (check for slang or informality)
- [ ] Warm but professional?
- [ ] Action-oriented?

### Before Sending
- [ ] Read aloud (catches awkward phrasing)
- [ ] Check recipient name spelling
- [ ] Verify dates and times are correct
- [ ] Confirm attachments if mentioned
- [ ] Check subject line is informative
- [ ] Wait 5 minutes and re-read (catch tone issues)
